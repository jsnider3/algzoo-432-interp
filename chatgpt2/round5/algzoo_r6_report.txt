ROUND 6 — DETERMINISTIC MECHANISTIC ACCURACY ESTIMATION FOR THE ALGZOO RNN
19 October 2026

Finding
=======

I did not find sampling parity. A deterministic two-level rule substantially
improves the matched z38 baseline: its interpolated-anchor version has about
6.28 times lower MSE with 8.92% less counted work. The development-selected
exact-S version has RMS error 0.225373 percentage points on development and
0.249308 on held-out networks. Its held-out MSE is about 222 times sampling's
under the declared arithmetic convention. Even the pure matrix-operation
lower bound for this implementation gives a ratio of 219.893.

The contribution is a reproducible improvement, a direct measurement of how
much correction amortization can lose, negative tests of low-budget rules and
a point/mean state recursion, and an explicit fixed-dimension lower bound for
a restricted Boolean-oracle model. None establishes impossibility for methods
with direct access to the 432 weights or for the average trained population.

Evaluation protocol and cost interpretation
==========================================

Development: population indices 0,16,...,1008. Held out: 8,24,...,1016.
Each has 64 networks. Every complete estimator also evaluates the six zoo
models, in order mystery, seed0, seed1, seed2, seed3, seed4. The larger
gate-history diagnostic uses eight development networks and the six zoo.
All numerical errors are estimate minus pop1k/reference.npz, in percentage
points. No new claimed score is over all 1,024 population members.

Correction settings and the selected high-compute combination were recorded
before held-out scoring in r6_results/correction_frozen.json. The held-out
curves pair the same methods selected on development. They are not a separate
held-out optimum. A later z38-combination exploratory check was confined to
development and did not improve the frozen choice.

Every estimator is deterministic on its own network's weights. Its nodes are
Gauss-Jacobi/Gauss-Legendre nodes, exact line pieces, or fixed arrangement
designs. There is no random or quasi-random estimator, accuracy regression,
calibration, importance sampling, or sampled correction. Random inputs occur
only in explicitly labeled diagnostics and verification.

One forward equivalent is 2,880 multiply-adds. For a constant cost C, the
sampling comparator in points is 100*sqrt(mean(p*(1-p))/C). When the measured
line-trace cost C_i varies by network, I instead use
100*sqrt(mean(p_i*(1-p_i)/C_i)). Mean C_i is shown on the horizontal axis.
This is the matched per-network-budget comparison, which need not equal
substitution of mean C_i into the constant-cost formula.

There are three kinds of cost in this report:

* Exact S-line anchors: the actual matrix/readout work counted by the supplied
  lines.trace_line. Scalar root finding, sorting and normal CDF work are not
  included. These are lower bounds for the implemented computation.
* Prefix-shared interpolation and event propagation: actual dense recurrent
  work plus an explicit scalar allowance, including 200 scalar equivalents
  per interval for root/CDF processing. This is an arithmetic convention,
  not a measured hardware upper bound.
* Correction grids: every actually evaluated forward row, plus a declared
  1% allowance for scalar processing. No hypothetical prefix speedup is used.

An exact-anchor combination includes that 1% correction allowance, so its
TOTAL reported count is an optimistic accounting estimate, not itself a
proved lower bound. The final JSON also gives a separate pure-MAC lower
bound, obtained by adding raw correction rows to exact-anchor MACs. For the
selected rule, the development/held pure-MAC costs are 2,468,385.792 and
2,474,720.588; sampling RMS upper bounds are 0.0172629 and 0.0168124 points;
the corresponding MSE-ratio lower bounds are 170.442 and 219.893. These bound
this implementation's work, not every possible optimized implementation.

1. Short audit
==============

z21 and z37 calculate dense S grids but attach the cost of an optimized exact
prefix-tree integrator absent from the archive. Their numerical integrals
can be useful; their advertised speed is conditional on that implementation.
At K=256, z21 actually evaluates 5,040*K rows per outer node versus its 9,300
charge, a factor 138.7. z37's corresponding factor is 120.6. Also, the MF2
path is curved because its smaller inputs change as mu(S); it cannot inherit
an affine MF3 S-line cost merely by arrangement-count scaling.

z18 has no factor-of-two error in its projected pair contribution. Its
symmetric pair tensor and factor one half are consistent. At q=8, a genuine
adjacent-pair implementation averages 241 forward rows, separation at most
two averages 411 2/3 (range 241-625), and all 15 pairs cost 1,009. The script
evaluates all pairs before masking, so those subset charges require actually
omitting the unwanted rows. Its additive-margin convolution also performs
weight-dependent threshold searches omitted from a forward-row-only count.

z38's row count and sampling comparator are consistent. Its failure remains
after the matched rerun reported below.

The reference RMS standard error is 0.02643 points on development and
0.02580 on held out. At 10^7 forwards, reference noise alone contributes a
raw MSE/sampling ratio near 10^7/2^20=9.54. A close parity claim there needs
a larger reference or an explicit reference-noise comparison. At the present
0.25-point errors, that noise is about 1% of squared error and cannot explain
the observed gap. Shared reference inputs correlate network errors, so the
64 networks do not automatically give 64 independent noise observations.

2. Arrangement amortization
==========================

I measured true-minus-anchor corrections using 256 independent draws in every
one of the 5,040 MF4 arrangements: 1,290,240 inputs per network, each evaluated
at truth, MF3 and MF4. The diagnostic therefore evaluates 3,870,720 forward
rows per network, before its scalar analysis. These draws do not enter an
estimator. Pooling seven appropriate MF4 strata supplies 1,792 draws for each
MF3 arrangement. Measurement variances retain the stratification.

After subtracting measurement variance, the median arrangement SD is 3.939
points for true-minus-MF3 and 2.048 for true-minus-MF4. Network 10th-90th
percentiles are 2.508-5.414 and 1.147-3.079. M,S positions explain median
21.9% and 16.9% of that variance; adding relative rank order explains 45.3%
and 34.9%. Thus the small fraction of total integrand variance attributed to
arrangements does not imply correction means agree within 0.05-0.1 points.

Noise-corrected RMS error of ideal subset averages versus all arrangements:

  MF3, 1 R position/class, 90 arrangements:   0.582 points, measurement SE .052
  MF3, 2 R positions/class, 180 arrangements: 0.281 points, measurement SE .035
  MF3, 4 R positions/class, 360 arrangements: 0.228 points, measurement SE .020
  MF4, 4 R,Q pairs/class, 360 arrangements:   0.266 points, measurement SE .058
  MF4, 8 R,Q pairs/class, 720 arrangements:   0.110 points, measurement SE .040
  MF4 arrangement coordinates, true-minus-MF3,
        8 pairs/class, 720 arrangements:     0.186 points, measurement SE .049

These are measurements granting exact value integration, not deployable
accuracy estimators and not lower bounds on all arrangement designs. The
0.110-point result does not resolve a sharp 0.100-point threshold.

The eight balanced pairs, expressed as ranks among the eight free positions
after fixing M and S, are (0,7),(7,0),(1,3),(3,1),(2,5),(5,2),(4,6),(6,4).
Each free position appears once as R and once as Q. Full zoo deviations,
their measurement errors, and per-network class results are in the JSON
appendix and the result bundle.

3. Two-level deterministic integration
=====================================

Let I_3 and I_4 denote correctness at their mean-field anchors. At an MF4
configuration define E_j as the q-node integral with only smaller input j
varied. The first-order indicator approximation is

  F_4 = clip(I_4 + sum_j(E_j-I_4), 0, 1).

The new estimator is

  p_hat = Q_fine^MF3(I_3) + Q_coarse^MF4(F_4-I_3).

The fine anchor is integrated over all 720 MF3 arrangements. In the selected
version it uses a 6x6 Beta outer rule and exact Gaussian S-line integration.
The preplanned companion uses the same outer rule with 32 S interpolation
segments. Its logit interpolant is affine in physical S; correctness is
integrated exactly for that interpolant. It is not an exact network trace.
Its recurrent work is shared over identical input prefixes using a token DAG.

The coarse correction uses the MF4 Beta coordinates, order two in each of
three outer dimensions, eight conditional-CDF S midpoints, eight lower-input
Gauss-Legendre nodes, and the eight balanced R,Q placements in every M,S
class. Every configuration evaluates 49 F_4 rows and one separate I_3 row.
The correction count is exactly

  8 outer nodes * 8 S points * 720 arrangements * 50 rows = 2,304,000 rows.

The declared correction charge is 2,327,040 after the 1% allowance. The
anchor is added separately. No final clipping is needed for these estimates.

The main table below provides all required statistics. The selected exact-S
rule gives RMS .225373 development / .249308 held out; the companion gives
.247840 / .247615. The direct z38 n3/K8/q8 baseline gives .621258 / .620461.
The companion's MSE is 6.28 times smaller than z38's at 8.92% less declared
cost; its MSE relative to sampling improves by roughly 6.9 times.

To isolate integration of the correction, I also grant a free reference
anchor. These are diagnostic advantages, not complete estimator scores:

  MF3 n1/K8/q4/all720: 167,040 rows; RMS .455/.468 development/held.
  MF3 n2/K16/q8/all720: 2,626,560 rows; RMS .360/.341.
  MF4 n1/K8/q8/all5040 minus MF4: 2,016,000 rows; RMS .162/.137.

The last row charges the extra I_3 diagnostic row actually evaluated. The
15.7-fold MF3 cost increase produces little improvement. Pure MF4 correction
is easier, but even it misses the requested 0.1-point partial result. The
full correction-statistic tables include means, sampling comparators, MSE
ratios and six zoo errors.

4. Distribution propagation and error geometry
==============================================

For the time recursion I implemented a deterministic point/mean component
law. It carries the full 16-dimensional hidden vector, M,S and both positions.
The max position is needed for a future handoff. At every represented state
the three event branches have their exact Gaussian probabilities. Branches
use either their exact truncated mean or a two-node conditional-CDF Gauss
rule. Optional merging groups by positions and CDF bins of M,S, replacing the
joint hidden vector by its weighted mean. The final input is integrated by
an exact gate sweep, including changes in the true second-max target.

At 200.6 counted forwards, the coarsest recursion has development/held RMS
4.983/5.090, against sampling 1.911/1.865. Without merging, the one-node rule
uses 18,394.9 forwards and still has RMS 5.256/5.057. Two nodes and eight
value bins cost 3,199.8 with RMS 5.579/5.260. Keeping more components did not
repair this closure. I stopped this implementation.

This is not the proposed exact affine-component law. I did not complete a
law that preserves every past-input dependency within an activation pattern.
The results reject the implemented point/mean closure; they do not refute
correlated affine mixtures or readout-aware merging.

As a feasibility diagnostic, 65,536 iid inputs per network were divided into
32,768 discovery and 32,768 independent coverage observations. On eight
development networks, median observed complete gate histories grow from
474 at time 3 to 7,774 at time 5, 20,980 at time 7, 38,936 at time 9 and
46,867 at time 10. The median test mass in unseen histories at time 10 is
68.84%; the top 1,024 discovery histories cover 12.76%. Keeping sixteen per
age pair covers 6.77%, and sixteen per event history covers 8.83%.

Fixing a gate pattern, the two true top positions and the predicted position
gives a particular convex polyhedral error-cone partition. There are a median
5,846 observed error fragments, yet 97.29% of independent test errors lie in
fragments absent from discovery. For the zoo, the unseen error-fragment
fractions are 97.52%,88.77%,86.83%,98.37%,98.38%,76.40%.

These measured fragments are not maximal convex error pieces. The request to
count all such maximal pieces needs a convention: even the union
{x<=0} union {y<=0} has continuously many maximal convex subcones
{a*x+b*y<=0}, a,b>0. A finite chosen partition, minimum convex cover or stated
merging algorithm is a well-defined alternative. I did not integrate the
full Gaussian masses of a merged error-cone cover, and do not assign the
diagnostic a fictitious accuracy-estimator cost or score.

5. The low-budget end and full curve
====================================

I evaluated six fixed MF2 product-Beta rules and six fixed MF3 prefix-shared
interpolation rules, along with the event recursions. MF2 n1/q2 costs 95.8
and has development/held RMS 7.770/7.413, against 2.765/2.699 for sampling.
MF3 n1/K2 costs 683.1 and gives 2.484/2.251, against 1.036/1.011. MF3
n2/K4 costs 5,448.7 and gives 1.174/1.232, against .367/.358. No measured
low-budget rule reaches parity. Pure anchors eventually retain their
mean-field bias even when their own integrals are accurate.

The included plot and frontier table select only by development error and
cost, then pair the held-out results. The measured range is about 48 to
3 million forwards. The sampling comparator extends to 10^7, but no new
algorithmic performance is extrapolated there. The original higher-cost
z38 measurements remain in the supplied archive. Every tested fixed rule
and all 72 preplanned anchor/correction combinations per split are retained.

6. Restricted lower bound and limits of the supplied floor arguments
===================================================================

The full proof follows in the appendix. For Boolean membership queries only,
consider one arbitrary polyhedral error cone in R^10 with Gaussian mass at
most q*=(1-sqrt(1-4*.069))/2=.074559052276. Its number of facets is unbounded.
Let G(r)=P[X_10 >= ||X_1:9||/r] and choose R=1.901649869866 with G(R)=q*.
Any deterministic adaptive N-query algorithm has worst-case absolute error
at least

  L_N = (2/5)*(G(R)-G(R*cos((256/(175*N))^(1/8)))).

This follows by constructing two indistinguishable inscribed polytopes in a
projective cross-section of the cone. The cap-union argument leaves four
fifths of directions with an unresolved radial shell. Half the resulting
Gaussian-mass gap is unavoidable. The lower bound is asymptotic to
.0397228583*N^(-1/4) in probability units, so worst-case MSE is Omega(N^-1/2).
This is a lower bound, not a matching rate theorem. Sampling MSE on the
same class is at most .069/N. At 10^4,10^5,10^6,10^7 queries the lower
bounds are .391097,.221534,.125048,.070462 points. Whole label-only affine
segment traces obey the same bound with N replaced by twice the number
of segments. Logits, activation masks and region coefficients are excluded.

A lower bound for a large class containing these RNNs does not lower-bound
the smaller RNN family: the minimax inequality goes in the opposite direction.
Nor does worst case imply the requested average over trained networks. The
theorem is consequently an obstruction to conic Boolean-oracle quadrature,
not a solution to the white-box impossibility question. No novelty claim
relative to the volume-estimation literature is needed for its use here.

Three narrower corrections are also useful:

* z34's first-order ANOVA margin projection error is not a universal additive
  accuracy floor. For iid Gaussian X,Y,Z let
  m=|X+Y|-|X-Y|+2|Z|. Its additive L2 projection is m_A=2|Z|, whose sign
  probability is one; m has sign probability 5/6. The different additive
  margin X+sqrt(3)|Z| has exactly that 5/6 sign probability. This analytic
  counterexample is continuous, positively homogeneous and piecewise linear.
  It does not supply an allowed calibrated estimator for these trained RNNs.
  A scheme converging to that particular ANOVA projection does inherit its
  sign-probability bias if there is no atom at zero, as the appendix proves.
* A certified unexplained mass u gives uncertainty interval [a,a+u] and
  optimal worst-case radius u/2. It does not force actual error u/2 on a
  known network. z26's tails are estimates, not certified masses, and its
  pattern counts on the nonlinear MF4 surface are not convex-piece counts.
* z22 establishes nonlocality for its margin proxy and finite S-grid test.
  Omitting a known correction contribution gives exactly its signed integral
  as truncation error. It does not turn the reported proxy contribution into
  a lower bound on every estimator of actual RNN accuracy.

7. Verdict and one next experiment
==================================

The requested breakthrough is still open. The new two-level computation
improves z38 materially and generalizes to the held-out cohort, but its cost
is roughly two orders of magnitude too high for its error. Neither the
0.1-point/10-times-anchor partial result nor a general white-box impossibility
theorem was obtained. Exact correlated state propagation and integration of
a compact merged error-cone cover remain untested possibilities here.

The single next experiment I would prioritize is exact S-integration of the
two-level correction. At each MF4 outer configuration, trace correctness
intervals for the 49 first-order streams and the MF3 baseline. Take the union
of every interval endpoint. Between adjacent endpoints the complete clamped
correction is constant, so its S-integral is an exact sum of Gaussian CDF
differences. Clipping must happen on each common interval; separately
integrating the streams and clipping their averages is a different model.
This includes off-boundary correction on constant-anchor lines and isolates
the unresolved error from the eight-point S grid.

I would freeze eight development networks and the same outer/arrangement
rule for a pilot. Continue only if exact S either reduces correction RMS by
at least 0.10 points or reduces charged correction work by at least fourfold.
Then use the requested partial-success gate: held-out correction RMS <=0.10
points at fully counted correction cost <=10 times the anchor. Full sampling
parity remains a separate end-to-end gate. This is a proposed experiment,
not a claimed achievable speedup or a prediction that it will bridge 220x.

Full lower-bound proof
======================

# Restricted lower bounds for the Gaussian accuracy problem

This note gives a self-contained lower bound, plus two smaller
statements tied to the supplied diagnostics. None is a lower bound on average
error over the 1,024 trained RNNs. The principal theorem concerns algorithms
whose information about an unknown classifier consists only of membership
answers, even if they choose queries adaptively and have unlimited free
computation between queries. It does not cover direct access to weights,
logits, activation masks, or affine coefficients.

## 1. The logical limitation must come first

Let F_RNN be the actual 432-parameter family, and let F_cone be a larger family
of conic classifiers containing F_RNN. A minimax lower bound for F_cone does
not imply the same bound for F_RNN. Indeed,

\[
\inf_A\sup_{f\in F_{\rm RNN}}|A(f)-I(f)|
\;\le\;
\inf_A\sup_{f\in F_{\rm cone}}|A(f)-I(f)|.
\]

The inequality goes in the unhelpful direction. Transferring a lower bound
would require constructing hard members *inside* the actual RNN family,
with the same access and cost model. Transferring it to the requested average
over trained networks would require an additional argument about the training
distribution or that particular finite population. Merely containing all
observed integrands is insufficient.

The theorem below therefore establishes a real obstruction to uniform
membership-based quadrature over a natural geometric class. It explains why
positive homogeneity and a conic description alone cannot supply a uniform
sampling-beating guarantee. It does not settle the requested white-box task.

## 2. Explicit theorem: one arbitrary polyhedral cone is enough

Let gamma_10 be standard Gaussian probability on R^10. Consider an unknown
polyhedral **error** cone E. The classifier is incorrect precisely on E; its
accuracy is 1 - gamma_10(E). Degenerate cones are allowed. Cone boundaries
have no effect on the integral, and we use closed-cone membership at boundary
queries.

Put

\[
q_* = \frac{1-\sqrt{1-4(0.069)}}2
      =0.074559052276\ldots.
\]

Restrict the class to cones with gamma_10(E) <= q_*. Every classifier in the
class then has accuracy at least 92.544 points, and sampling with N independent
examples has MSE at most 0.069/N. This choice aligns the comparator with the
population benchmark; it does not assert that the hard cones are trained RNNs.

Define

\[
G(r)=\Pr\{X_{10}\ge \|X_{1:9}\|/r\},\qquad r>0.
\]

For alpha = arctan(r), direct spherical integration gives the elementary form

\[
G(r)=\frac{35\alpha-28\sin(2\alpha)+7\sin(4\alpha)
 -(4/3)\sin(6\alpha)+(1/8)\sin(8\alpha)}{35\pi}.
\]

Let R be the unique radius with G(R) = q_*. Numerically,
R = 1.901649869866. For an integer N >= 1 set

\[
\theta_N=\left(\frac{256}{175N}\right)^{1/8},\qquad
L_N=\frac25\,[G(R)-G(R\cos\theta_N)].
\]

**Theorem.** Any deterministic algorithm using at most N membership queries
has worst-case absolute error at least L_N on this class. Equivalently, for
every eta > 0, some admissible polyhedral cone gives error at least L_N - eta.
The same conclusion holds if the algorithm estimates accuracy rather than
error probability.

Numerically:

| Membership queries N | Worst-case absolute-error lower bound (points) | Sampling RMS upper bound (points) | Squared lower bound / sampling MSE |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 1.179407 | 2.626785 | 0.202 |
| 1,000 | 0.685273 | 0.830662 | 0.681 |
| 10,000 | 0.391097 | 0.262679 | 2.217 |
| 100,000 | 0.221534 | 0.083066 | 7.113 |
| 1,000,000 | 0.125048 | 0.026268 | 22.662 |
| 10,000,000 | 0.070462 | 0.008307 | 71.955 |

These are **minimax bounds for hypothetical members of the stated geometric
class**, not measured errors or RMS results on development, held-out, or zoo
networks. One ordinary Boolean correctness query could be supplied by one
forward pass, but an actual forward also exposes logits. An algorithm that
uses those extra data is outside the theorem.

As N grows,

\[
L_N=(0.0397228583\ldots+o(1))N^{-1/4}
\]

in probability units. Thus its worst-case squared error is Omega(N^-1/2) in this
oracle model, whereas sampling MSE is at most 0.069/N. No growing
number K of convex cones is needed: E itself is one convex polyhedral cone.
Its number of facets is not bounded, which is essential to interpreting the
result.

### Full proof

**Projective cross-section.** For a compact convex B subset R^9 define

\[
C(B)=\{t(z,1):t>0,\ z\in B\}\cup\{0\}.
\]

When B is a polytope, C(B) is a finitely generated polyhedral cone. A Gaussian
change of variables (y,t) = (tz,t), t > 0, gives

\[
\gamma_{10}(C(B))
 =\frac{12}{\pi^5}\int_B(1+\|z\|^2)^{-5}\,dz.
\tag{1}
\]

To check the constant, integrate
(2 pi)^-5 t^9 exp[-t^2(1+||z||^2)/2] over positive t; the result is
12 pi^-5 (1+||z||^2)^-5. This density depends only on ||z||. If B_r is the
9-dimensional Euclidean ball of radius r, (1) equals G(r).

The displayed elementary formula for G follows independently by writing the
Gaussian direction in polar angle alpha from the positive tenth axis:

\[
G(r)=\frac{128}{35\pi}\int_0^{\arctan r}\sin^8(t)\,dt.
\]

**An adversarial transcript.** Run the algorithm against membership in the
circular cone C(B_R). This reference cone is not polyhedral; that is harmless,
because we will exhibit two polyhedral cones realizing its entire finite
query transcript. Every positive query with positive tenth coordinate maps
to a point z_i in B_R. Let

\[
P_- = \operatorname{conv}(\{0\}\cup\{z_i:\text{positive queries}\}).
\]

There are at most N nonzero generators. C(P_-) agrees with the transcript:
it contains every positive queried point and is a subset of C(B_R), so it
contains no negative queried point. Queries at or below the hyperplane
x_10 = 0 cause no difficulty: all these cones contain the origin and no other
point of that hyperplane, and contain no point with x_10 < 0.

**Missing directions.** A spherical cap of angular radius theta on the unit
sphere S^8 has normalized area

\[
\frac{35}{32}\int_0^\theta\sin^7(t)\,dt
\;\le\;\frac{35}{256}\theta^8.
\tag{2}
\]

Here theta_N < pi/2 for every N >= 1. Place a cap of radius theta_N around
the direction of each nonzero z_i. By the union bound and (2), all the caps
together cover at most

\[
N\frac{35}{256}\theta_N^8=\frac15
\]

of S^8. At least four fifths of directions u are consequently at angular
distance at least theta_N from every z_i. For such u,

\[
\sup_{z\in P_-}u\cdot z\le R\cos\theta_N.
\]

Therefore every point ru on that ray with
R cos(theta_N) < r <= R is outside P_-. Because the density in (1) is radial,
the omitted cone measure is at least

\[
\gamma_{10}(C(B_R))-\gamma_{10}(C(P_-))
\;\ge\;\frac45[G(R)-G(R\cos\theta_N)].
\tag{3}
\]

**A second polyhedral realization.** Take increasingly dense finite sets of
points on the sphere of radius R and let P_+ be their convex hull together
with P_-. Such an inscribed polytope contains P_-, is contained in B_R, and
can have cone measure arbitrarily close to G(R). For a concrete dense family,
normalize all nonzero integer vectors in [-M,M]^9 to length R and let M grow.
Every finite P_+ is a polytope. It contains all positive queries and, being
inside B_R, excludes all negative queries. Both resulting error cones have
mass at most q_*.

For any eta > 0, choose this approximation so that the two legal cones have
measures differing by at least

\[
\frac45[G(R)-G(R\cos\theta_N)]-2\eta.
\]

The algorithm makes the same adaptive queries, receives the same answers,
and returns the same number on both cones. At least one of the two errors
is half their measure difference or larger. This proves L_N - eta, and hence
the stated supremum lower bound. Adaptivity and unlimited intermediate
computation do not change the argument.

### Whole affine-line traces do not remove this oracle obstruction

Suppose a query reveals the entire correctness function on an affine line,
or a path containing finitely many affine segments. Count L segments in
total, including adaptively chosen segments. The theorem holds with N = 2L.

To see this, project the portion of a segment with x_10 > 0 to the hyperplane
x_10 = 1. It lies on an affine line or is a single point. Its intersection
with B_R is an interval, possibly empty, and its closure has at most two
endpoints. Form P_- from all those endpoints and 0. Convexity makes P_-
contain every positive point of every queried segment. Since it lies in
B_R, it also excludes every negative point. The same P_+ construction
preserves all complete segment traces. The missing-cap proof uses at most
2L endpoint directions.

This is a stronger oracle than isolated correctness evaluation, but it
still reveals only labels along paths. It does not reveal the network's
linear maps or activation-region inequalities. The result is in numbers of
segments, not an independently proved conversion from arithmetic work to
forward-pass equivalents.

## 3. What a certified region tail really implies

Suppose a method has integrated correctness exactly on a known set U and
knows that the unclassified remainder has probability u. Let a be the
known correct mass in U. If it has no further information about the labels
outside U, the possible total accuracy lies in

\[
[a,a+u].
\]

**Certificate proposition.** The smallest possible worst-case absolute
error based on that information is u/2, attained by a + u/2. No smaller
uniform guarantee is possible because the two completions "all remaining
points wrong" and "all remaining points right" produce the same information
and totals separated by u.

This proposition concerns an information restriction or the radius of an
uncertainty certificate. It does **not** say that every estimator has actual
error at least u/2 on the known network. A fallback may guess correctly;
different signed errors may cancel; the weights may provide a global
constraint on the unvisited regions. Those cases supply additional
information or do not require a worst-case guarantee.

The supplied `pop1k/z26_seed0.txt` reports the following estimated median
tails. If they were exact unexplained tails, their midpoint certificate
radii would compare with sampling as follows:

| Patterns per arrangement | Assumed build cost | Estimated tail (points) | Midpoint certificate radius (points) | Sampling RMS (points) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 5,040 | 80.103 | 40.0515 | 0.3700 |
| 10 | 50,400 | 33.667 | 16.8335 | 0.1170 |
| 30 | 151,200 | 15.146 | 7.5730 | 0.0676 |
| 100 | 504,000 | 4.989 | 2.4945 | 0.0370 |
| 300 | 1,512,000 | 1.299 | 0.6495 | 0.0214 |
| 1,000 | 5,040,000 | 0.291 | 0.1455 | 0.0117 |

At 1,000 patterns per arrangement, even the optimal interval midpoint has
an illustrative radius about 12.4 times sampling's RMS. This says that a
proof consisting only of covering the most massive activation patterns and
declaring the rest unknown is too weak at the measured coverage. It says
nothing comparable about the actual error of a good fallback.

Three qualifications are essential:

1. The costs count only the assumed one-forward construction per pattern.
   Discovery and integration are additional work.
2. `z26.py` measures 40 arrangements for 16 population networks plus the zoo,
   using sampled frequencies and a Good–Turing estimate. Its tails are not
   rigorous certificates, despite the printed label "guaranteed". Nor is a
   median tail an RMS statistic for all 1,024 networks.
3. It counts distinct activation bit patterns on the MF4 image. The map
   setting lower inputs to mu(Q) is nonlinear, so a convex activation cone's
   preimage in retained-value coordinates need not be convex or connected.
   The count is not automatically the number of connected pieces of the
   anchor domain, and it is not a count of convex components of the true
   error set.

## 4. What non-local correction mass really implies

Write the true conditional correction as

\[
c(z)=\Pr(\text{correct}\mid z)-I_0(z),
\]

and let B be a set of top-k configurations discarded by a proposed local
correction. If its complement is integrated exactly but correction on B is
set to zero, the accuracy error is exactly

\[
-\int_B c(z)\,d\nu(z).
\tag{4}
\]

Thus a boundary-only truncation needs either a bound on that omitted
integral, or a mechanism for computing it. Boundary smoothness by itself
does not control it. Equation (4) is an exact truncation identity, not a
universal lower bound on every boundary-based estimator: errors on the
complement might cancel the omission.

In `z22_seed0.txt`, the median contribution on lines with no detected anchor
crossing is -0.362 points for the *first-order additive-margin proxy*. Dropping
that contribution while evaluating the proxy on other lines exactly would
give a +0.362-point median error relative to that proxy's full integral.
At that error size, the sampling crossover is only

\[
C=0.069/(0.00362)^2\simeq 5,265
\]

forwards. This is useful evidence against that particular truncation.

It is not a 0.362-point lower bound for true RNN accuracy. `z22.py` uses the
first-order margin approximation, and "no crossing" means no sign change
between 64 midpoint S-grid values. It does not call `lines.trace_line` to
certify the absence of narrow extra crossings. The supplied "68%" is a
diagnostic of that sampled proxy calculation, not a theorem about true
correction mass.

## 5. "Count the maximal convex pieces" needs a definition

Even a union of two polyhedral cones can have infinitely many maximal convex
subsets. In R^2 take

\[
E=\{x\le0\}\cup\{y\le0\}.
\]

For every a,b > 0, the half-plane cone

\[
H_{a,b}=\{ax+by\le0\}
\]

is contained in E and is a maximal convex subset of E. To prove maximality,
take any p in E outside H_{a,b}, so a p_x + b p_y = s > 0. Choose z with both
coordinates strictly positive and a z_x + b z_y = s/2. Then w = 2z - p lies
on the boundary of H_{a,b}, but z = (p+w)/2 is outside E. Adding p to a convex
set containing H_{a,b} would therefore leave E. Varying a/b gives a continuum
of distinct maximal convex cones.

Useful finite objectives include a specified disjoint polyhedral partition,
the size and integration cost of a particular convex cover, or a minimum
convex-cover problem with a stated admissible representation. Greedy merging
of adjacent cones is another concrete algorithm, but its final number can
depend on merge order. The activation partition is one canonical starting
point; its size alone does not lower-bound the best error-set representation.

## 6. The ideal-ANOVA experiment is not a lower bound for additive accuracy estimators

`z34.py` calls its result a bound for every additive lower-input model, because
the first-order ANOVA projection minimizes squared error of the **margin**.
That implication is false: minimizing squared margin error does not minimize
error in the integrated sign probability.

Here is an explicit counterexample with independent standard Gaussian inputs
and a continuous, piecewise-linear, positively homogeneous margin, so it does
not rely on discontinuous or non-ReLU-compatible functions. Let

\[
m(X,Y,Z)=|X+Y|-|X-Y|+2|Z|.
\]

The first two terms have conditional mean zero given any one coordinate:
for fixed X, replacing Y by -Y reverses their sign, and vice versa. Therefore
the first-order ANOVA projection is

\[
m_A(X,Y,Z)=2|Z|,
\]

which predicts P(m_A > 0) = 1. On the other hand,

\[
|X+Y|-|X-Y|
=2\operatorname{sign}(XY)\min(|X|,|Y|).
\]

The true margin is negative exactly when XY < 0 and |Z| is smaller than
both |X| and |Y|. Independent Gaussian signs give the first event probability
1/2; exchangeable continuous magnitudes give the second probability 1/3,
independently. Hence P(m > 0) = 5/6.

Now use the additive homogeneous margin

\[
g(X,Y,Z)=X+\sqrt3|Z|.
\]

It has P(g > 0) = 5/6 exactly: the incorrect region X <= -sqrt(3)|Z| is a
wedge of angle pi/3 in the rotationally invariant (X,Z) plane. Thus the
L2-optimal additive margin has accuracy bias 1/6, while another additive
margin has zero integrated accuracy bias. All equalities at zero have
probability zero here.

This does not construct an improved estimator for the supplied RNNs. It
refutes the proposed general inference from ANOVA optimality. The measured
0.074-point ANOVA bias is a useful diagnostic of one approximation, not a
proved floor for all additive lower-input estimators, still less for all
deterministic mechanistic estimators.

The experiment also approximates the conditional expectations using finitely
many background draws. Removing variance from the reported scoring statistic
does not by itself remove the sign-probability bias introduced by noisy
conditional-mean estimates.

There is a precise restricted statement that *is* true. If g_n converges to
m_A in L2 and P(m_A = 0) = 0, then P(g_n > 0) converges to P(m_A > 0).
For every delta > 0,

\[
|\Pr(g_n>0)-\Pr(m_A>0)|
\le \Pr(|m_A|\le\delta)
  +\frac{\mathbb E[(g_n-m_A)^2]}{\delta^2}.
\]

First take n to infinity, then delta to zero. Thus a refinement scheme
specifically converging to that projection ultimately inherits its bias.
This is an asymptotic statement about that target approximation, not a
lower bound for finite truncations or other additive constructions.

## 7. The fixed-size circuit claim should be softened

The assertion that any white-box lower bound here "would be a concrete
circuit lower bound" is too categorical. It first needs a computational
model, allowed primitives, weight precision, and target accuracy. A
fixed-precision Boolean-circuit formulation is one possible model, not a
consequence of the question as posed. Restricted arithmetic programs,
decision trees, region certificates, and path-oracle algorithms give other
models with different lower-bound questions.

Likewise, exact accuracy need not have a finite expression in a stipulated
arithmetic instruction set. A region-based method can target arbitrarily
small certified error, given enough computation; calling every such
calculation literally exact hides its integration and precision costs.

There is a further obstacle for an unrestricted bound averaged over a fixed
finite population: with unconstrained offline preprocessing, a lookup table
can encode all those answers. The question explicitly excludes fitted
prediction, so that construction is inadmissible here, but a theorem would
need to formalize the admissible mechanistic computations to enforce the
exclusion. This is a modeling issue before it is a circuit-complexity claim.

The supportable verdict is consequently narrow but substantive: deterministic
Boolean-oracle quadrature has a proved obstruction even for single convex
Gaussian cones; coverage-only certificates are far too loose at the reported
tails; and the available locality diagnostic does not justify discarding the
off-boundary correction. None proves the 432-weight white-box population
challenge impossible.

## Reproduction

`r6_bounds_check.py` uses only Python's standard `math` library to reproduce
the theorem's constants, numerical table, region-tail comparison, and the
truncation crossover. It contains no target-network evaluation, data fitting,
randomness, or use of the population reference as an estimator.

The prompt explicitly discloses the missing optimized tree, so this is a conditional implementation assumption, not a concealed bug. The MF3/MF4 exact-line cost must be reproduced, including preprocessing, before presenting the curves as achieved computational performance. Under the supplied code, the MF3 anchor wins disappear. For example its order-4 cost is 2,949,120 rather than 24,448 forwards, so 0.127 points RMS is about 61× sampling MSE rather than approximately 0.51×. Some accurate MF2 anchor rules can still beat sampling of the original task at much larger actual costs, but they do not estimate true accuracy.

2. **MF2 has an additional invalid cost extrapolation.** In `z37.py:39–42`, all eight lower inputs equal μ(S), which varies nonlinearly as S changes. Thus the input path is not an affine S-line, even inside a fixed activation pattern. The MF3/MF4 affine line tracer does not apply unchanged. Pro-rating the MF3 tree cost by the number of arrangements does not establish a 191-forward MF2 node. A curve/root integrator could handle the μ(S) path, but its implementation and cost need separate accounting.

3. **The population reference is noisy enough to dominate high-budget parity tests.** Reading `pop1k/reference.npz` gives RMS accuracy SE 0.02567 percentage points overall, 0.02643 on development (indices 0 mod 16), and 0.02580 on held out (indices 8 mod 16). Its mean p(1-p) values are respectively 0.0691045, 0.0732678, and 0.0698015. Raw squared error against this reference includes its noise. The reference alone contributes an MSE ratio C/2²⁰ against a nominal C-forward sampling baseline: 0.954 at C=10⁶ and 9.537 at C=10⁷. Report raw MSE and a noise-adjusted estimate `mean((estimate-reference)**2 - se_acc**2)`; alternatively compare raw MSE to `mean(p*(1-p)/C + se_acc**2)`. Close parity at large C needs larger independent references. A common input set correlates network errors, so increasing the number of networks does not provide 1,024 independent reference-noise replicates. This is immaterial to estimates missing by several percentage points.

4. **The old anchor references are adequate for their main conclusions, but not uniform across the table.** `z21.py:47–56` correctly computes the binomial reference SE. The K=64 and K=256 orders 4–6 logs use 2²² inputs (about 0.014 points SE); the K=512 orders 6–8 and order 9–10 logs use 2²⁶ (about 0.003 points). Thus the task summary's blanket 0.003-point description does not apply to all listed rows. `z37.py:46–53` and `z38.py:46–51` use 2²⁴ references, whose noise is too small to change their unfavorable accuracy conclusions. No reference evaluation should be charged to a deployed estimator.

5. **The sampling comparator formulas are correct, with different estimands explicitly separated.** `z21.py:56,65` uses the MF4 anchor's p(1-p), appropriate when scoring its integral against sampled anchor accuracy. `z37.py:53,69` and `z38.py:51,57–59` use true accuracy p(1-p). The latter is correct for the requested accuracy baseline. For an anchor-only claim, note that MF2 is substantially easier even for plain sampling: on the 16-network subset its mean anchor p(1-p) is only 0.4043 of true accuracy's, whereas MF3 and MF4 are 0.9743 and 0.9659. Use each evaluated cohort's mean p(1-p), rather than substituting 0.069 for every cohort. Marginal plug-in bias in p(1-p) is negligible at these reference sizes.

## Rows, convolution, and projection

- **No double counting error in z18's cut-model algebra.** The rows are one anchor, 6q singleton rows, and q² rows for each unordered pair (`z18.py:53–59,63–68`). The symmetric D array stores each pair in both orientations (`z18.py:67`); the division by two in the constant (`z18.py:80`) correctly compensates. For q=8, the all-pair model needs 1,009 rows. Uniformly averaging over the 210 possible sets of six lower positions gives exactly 3 adjacent pairs and 17/3 pairs at distance at most two. Therefore the selected variants need 241 and 411⅔ forward rows on average; the gap≤2 count ranges from 241 to 625. The script evaluates all 15 pairs before masking (`z18.py:56–59,77–83`), so its executed experiment is costlier than a deployed selected-pair implementation. Omitting unused pair rows is a straightforward legitimate optimization. The nin sampled rows (`z18.py:60–61`) are measurement work, not estimator work, and should not be added to its budget.
- **The 241–412 counts omit target-dependent postprocessing.** `conv_p` (`z18.py:38–45`) has q⁴ threshold queries into a sorted list of q² pair sums. At q=8 that is 4,096 queries (roughly 24,576 binary-search comparisons); at q=16 it is 65,536 (roughly 524,288 comparisons). Counting additions and multiplications as scalar arithmetic operations, its basic arithmetic is approximately `4*q**4 + 4*q**2 - 2`, or 2.89 and 45.69 forward equivalents at 5,760 scalar operations per forward, before sorting, search, indexing, and pair projection. Comparison/search work needs an explicit cost convention or timing; it is not free. This is a modest correction at q=8 but grows quickly with q. Count only the convolution for the chosen estimator, not all six compared variants.
- **The convolution is exact for the stated finite additive model.** Pairing into A, B, C and using `searchsorted(..., right=True)` computes P(m>0), with the product atom weights correctly restored. It does not make the cut-model approximation exact for the actual network. Strict positive margin also excludes ties where the target is the lowest tied index; retained implementations should handle ties consistently if they occur at quadrature atoms. No measured conclusion-changing tie case was found in this audit.
- **z18's conditional sampling-noise correction is correct.** `p_hat*(1-p_hat)/(nin-1)` (`z18.py:85,90`) is an unbiased estimator of the variance of the binomial conditional mean. The reported 0.048-point integrated RMS is a noise-subtracted measurement of the integrated model error, not the result of a deterministic outer integrator. The appended all-population log gives raw RMS 0.064 and estimated measurement noise 0.043 points. Its generating postprocessing and per-configuration saved arrays are absent, so that last subtraction cannot be independently reconstructed from the included program alone.
- **z38's estimator forward count is internally correct.** `z38.py:38–45,57` uses exactly one base row plus 7q singleton rows per arrangement and S-node, yielding `n*n*720*K*(1+7*q)`. No extra factor of seven, duplicate anchor, or charged reference is present. Weight-dependent aggregation is small and only raises the cost. Its reported failure by thousands in MSE therefore survives the audit.

## Geometry/Markov observation for follow-up

Exact propagation of `(h_t,M_t,S_t,pos(S_t))` shares prefixes but does not automatically merge distributions. Different old inputs with equal ages and events generally induce different hidden-state laws. A mixture component should therefore preserve joint affine dependence on old inputs and its truncation/gating domain; moment-only merging needs a new approximation analysis. One promising tractable diagnostic is to partition the next-input axis by *every gate threshold plus M and S* at each deterministic state, and integrate each interval analytically or by a very low-order positive Gaussian-CDF rule. This preserves correlations within each propagated point and shares time prefixes. However, point representatives reset affine dependence and component counts can grow exponentially, so its budget/error curve is an experiment, not a proved replacement for the suggested affine-state recursion.

For error-cone enumeration, counting activation regions is insufficient: neighboring activation regions with the same affine or compatible error decision can merge, while one activation region can have several task/output pieces. Any measured lower-bound surrogate must count error-relevant pieces and their mass, or constrain the algorithm to a particular region representation. The 0.074-point error of the ideal first-order ANOVA *margin* projection is not a lower bound on the integrated accuracy error of every additive margin model: it minimizes squared margin error, not the signed accuracy functional.

# Gate histories and error-cone fragments: measurement diagnostic

`r6_geometry.py` is a guarded NumPy program using at most two BLAS threads. The run in `r6_geometry.json` uses 65,536 independent standard-Gaussian sequences, the eight development networks 0, 128, ..., 896, and the six zoo models. The first 32,768 sequences discover patterns and rank them by frequency; the remaining 32,768 measure their coverage independently. All work is measurement, not a proposed deterministic accuracy estimator. No target accuracy or population calibration is fitted.

## Prefix proliferation

Medians over the eight development networks:

| Time | Observed gate histories | Test mass in previously unseen histories | Test mass captured by the 1,024 most frequent discovery histories |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 474 | 0.18% | 99.82% |
| 5 | 7,774 | 8.90% | 77.73% |
| 7 | 20,980 | 27.76% | 46.22% |
| 9 | 38,936 | 55.74% | 19.65% |
| 10 | 46,867 | 68.84% | 12.76% |

At time 10, individual networks have 22,548–58,256 observed histories. The 90 ordered max/second-max age pairs do not make those histories interchangeable: selecting the 16 most frequent histories within each age pair captures a median 6.77% of test input mass. Selecting 16 within each exact event history captures 8.83%. There are 6,450 observed event histories in the common input set. These grouped figures restrict the number retained per group; the ungrouped top-1,024 list allocates its capacity differently, so its greater coverage is consistent.

This is evidence against a recursion that retains only a small finite list of complete gate histories. It does not bound a method that merges histories with equivalent future behavior, or approximates their joint state distributions successfully. Merely counting gate histories can overcount distinct current affine maps and error-relevant states.

## Error-cone partition

For a fixed full activation pattern, all recurrent states and final logits are linear in the ten inputs. Also fix the positions of the largest and second-largest inputs and the network's predicted position. The gate conditions, the top-two conditions, and all output comparisons are homogeneous linear inequalities. Their intersection is a convex polyhedral cone. When the predicted position differs from the true second-largest position, it is an error-cone fragment.

There are a median **5,846 observed error-cone fragments**, with range **2,405–6,806**, in the eight development networks. A median **97.29% of test errors lie in fragments absent from the discovery half**. Even ignoring top-two and output labels, a median 92.79% of test errors have previously unseen full activation histories. For the zoo, the unseen error-fragment fractions are 97.52%, 88.77%, 86.83%, 98.37%, 98.38%, and 76.40% (mystery, seeds 0–4).

These are **not maximal convex error pieces**. Neighboring activation or output regions may merge into a larger convex error set; the diagnostic neither finds such mergers nor proves they are impossible. The large missing mass shows that a list of observed local fragments has poor coverage. A geometry-based improvement would need to construct much larger error regions or integrate families of fragments together.

9. Accounting, accuracy, and interpretation
------------------------------------------
One full dense RNN forward is defined as 2880 MACs. Shared-prefix methods
count the dense arithmetic they actually execute, normalized by 2880.
They do not claim the original unimplemented 9300- or 1500-forward trees.
The exact-anchor code sums the costs returned by supplied lines.trace_line
on all 720 arrangements at every outer node. Only overflowing lines retry
with doubled capacity; all discarded attempts are included in that count.
Those exact-line counts omit scalar gate roots, sorting, CDFs, and related
work, and therefore form MAC-model lower bounds for this implementation.

Other methods use declared scalar-work allowances, not measured hardware
upper bounds. Examples are 200 scalar-equivalent operations per root/CDF
interval and the correction convention cost=1.01*forward_rows. Allocation,
bookkeeping, and library internals are not individually timed or converted
to exact MAC equivalents. Wall-clock seconds are supplementary only.

The selected MF4 correction evaluates 2,304,000 complete forward rows per
network: 8 outer nodes * 720 arrangements * 8 S nodes * 50 rows. The 50 are
one MF4 base, 6*8 lower-input rows, and one separately evaluated MF3 anchor.
All repeated rows are charged as executed. The fine MF3 anchor cost is added;
there is no free subtraction or uncharged duplicate anchor. Subset reports
charge the subset's deployment cost, not the cost of the larger diagnostic
run from which its already computed integrals were extracted.

An exact-anchor combination includes actual anchor MACs plus the correction
1% allowance. Its reported convention cost is optimistic but is NOT a
proved total lower bound: cost_is_lower_bound=false and
exact_anchor_omits_scalar_work=true express this. Only the separate
mac_only_cost_lower_bound field (anchor MAC count + raw correction rows)
is the MAC-model bound. sampling_rms_at_mac_cost and mse_ratio_at_mac_cost
use that bound. These implementation counts do not rule out an optimized
algorithm that has not been implemented or tested here.

Accuracy errors are estimator minus supplied reference, in percentage
points. The reference uses 1,048,576 measurement samples; it is not exact
ground truth. Reported estimator RMS values retain this reference noise.
Sampling curves use the supplied reference accuracy p in p*(1-p)/C. When
costs vary by network, the comparison averages p_i*(1-p_i)/C_i before taking
the square root, rather than substituting one mean cost. The horizontal
reference-noise line is a measurement scale, not a universal error floor.

The correction JSON panels named correction_with_reference_anchor and
correction_with_reference_mf4 use a measured reference anchor to diagnose
the correction alone. They are not deployable estimators and are excluded
from the primary frontier. All actual rules use the target network weights,
fixed quadrature/design choices, and their own calculated anchors. No RNG,
QMC sequence, population regression, or held-out calibration enters them.

```python
"""CPU deterministic MF3 quadrature with shared prefixes and linear-logit roots.
Run from this repository. All rules are fixed; reference values only score them.
"""
import argparse, itertools, json, time
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
from scipy.special import ndtr, ndtri, roots_jacobi
from threadpoolctl import threadpool_limits

ARR = np.array(list(itertools.permutations(range(10), 3)))

def load(ids):
    z = np.load('pop1k/seed0.npz')
    keys = ['Wih', 'Whh', 'Wo']
    pop = [z['s1048576_' + k] for k in keys]
    zoo = []
    for f in ['weights.npz'] + [f'weights_seed{i}.npz' for i in range(5)]:
        w = np.load(f)
        zoo.append([w['rnn.weight_ih_l0'][:, 0],
                    w['rnn.weight_hh_l0'], w['linear.weight']])
    return [np.ascontiguousarray(np.stack([
        pop[j][i] if i < 1024 else zoo[i-1024][j] for i in ids
    ]), dtype=np.float64) for j in range(3)]

def rule(n, a=1, b=1):
    x, w = roots_jacobi(n, b-1, a-1)
    return (x+1)/2, w/w.sum()

def forward(W, X):
    wi, wh, wo = W
    h = np.zeros((len(wi), len(X), 16))
    for t in range(10):
        h = np.maximum(h @ wh.transpose(0, 2, 1)
                       + wi[:, None, :] * X[None, :, t, None], 0)
    return h @ wo.transpose(0, 2, 1)

def prefix_forward(W, tokens, values):
    """Evaluate exactly the same rows, deduplicating identical input prefixes.
    Returns logits and actual dense multiply/add work, in 2880-MAC units.
    Also charges scalar adds and ReLU comparisons, unlike the base MAC count.
    """
    wi, wh, wo = W
    drive = wi[:, None, :] * values[None, :, None]
    h = np.zeros((len(wi), 1, 16))
    inv = np.zeros(len(tokens), dtype=int)
    work = 16*len(values)
    for t in range(10):
        edges, inv = np.unique(np.column_stack([inv, tokens[:, t]]),
                               axis=0, return_inverse=True)
        if t == 0:
            h = np.maximum(drive[:, edges[:, 1]], 0)
            work += len(edges)*16
        else:
            h = np.maximum(h[:, edges[:, 0]] @ wh.transpose(0, 2, 1)
                           + drive[:, edges[:, 1]], 0)
            work += len(edges)*(256+32)
    L = h @ wo.transpose(0, 2, 1)
    work += len(h[0])*160
    return L[:, inv], work/2880

def line_probability(L, s, y):
    """Gaussian measure of correctness for logits linearly interpolated in x_S.
    The integral of this interpolant is exact; the network need not be affine.
    """
    target = np.take_along_axis(L, y[None, :, None, None], axis=-1)
    d = target-L
    d0, dd = d[:, :, :-1], np.diff(d, axis=2)
    nz = dd != 0
    r = np.divide(-d0, dd, out=np.zeros_like(dd), where=nz)
    low = np.maximum(0, np.max(np.where(dd > 0, r, -np.inf), axis=-1))
    high = np.minimum(1, np.min(np.where(dd < 0, r, np.inf), axis=-1))
    bad = np.any((~nz) & ((d0 < 0) | ((d0 == 0) &
        (np.arange(10)[None, None, None, :] < y[None, :, None, None]))), axis=-1)
    low, high = np.clip(low, 0, 1), np.clip(high, 0, 1)
    a = s[:-1]+np.diff(s)*low
    b = s[:-1]+np.diff(s)*high
    mass = np.where((high > low) & ~bad, ndtr(b)-ndtr(a), 0)
    return mass.sum(axis=-1)/(ndtr(s[-1])-ndtr(s[0]))

def mf3(W, n, K):
    """n x n Beta outer rule; K affine interpolation intervals along S."""
    est = np.zeros(len(W[0])); cost = 0.0
    ur, wr = rule(n, 8, 3); ug, wg = rule(n, 2, 1)
    for a, wa in zip(ur, wr):
        for g, w in zip(ug, wg):
            b = a+(1-a)*g
            R, M = ndtri(a), ndtri(b)
            s = ndtri(a+(b-a)*np.arange(K+1)/K)
            mu = -np.exp(-R*R/2)/np.sqrt(2*np.pi)/a
            # symbols 0=M, 1=R, 2=mu; interior S nodes have distinct symbols.
            vals = np.r_[M, R, mu, s[1:-1]]
            st = np.r_[1, np.arange(3, K+2), 0]
            tok = np.full((720, K+1, 10), 2, dtype=int)
            for j in range(720):
                tok[j, :, ARR[j, 0]] = 0
                tok[j, :, ARR[j, 1]] = st
                tok[j, :, ARR[j, 2]] = 1
            L, c = prefix_forward(W, tok.reshape(-1, 10), vals)
            p = line_probability(L.reshape(len(W[0]), 720, K+1, 10),
                                 s, ARR[:, 1])
            est += wa*w*p.mean(axis=1)
            # 200 scalar-equivalent operations/interval: margins, roots,
            # reductions, masks and both normal CDFs. Report separately.
            cost += c+720*K*200/2880
    return est, cost

def score(est, ids, cost):
    ref = np.load('pop1k/reference.npz')
    p = ref['acc'][ids]; err = (est-p)*100
    ans = {'cost': float(cost), 'ids': list(map(int, ids)),
           'estimates': est.tolist(), 'errors_pp': err.tolist()}
    for name, mask in [('dev', ids < 1024),
                       ('held', (ids < 1024) & (ids % 16 == 8))]:
        if name == 'dev': mask &= ids % 16 == 0
        if mask.any():
            e = err[mask]; samp = 100*np.sqrt(np.mean(p[mask]*(1-p[mask]))/cost)
            ans[name] = dict(rms=float(np.sqrt(np.mean(e*e))),
                             mean=float(e.mean()), sampling=float(samp),
                             ratio=float(np.mean(e*e)/samp**2))
    ans['zoo_errors'] = err[ids >= 1024].tolist()
    return ans

def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument('--split', choices=['dev', 'held', 'all'], default='dev')
    ap.add_argument('--settings', default='1:1,1:2,2:4,3:8,4:16,6:32')
    ap.add_argument('--out', default='r6_results/anchors_dev.json')
    a = ap.parse_args()
    ids = np.r_[np.arange(0, 1024, 16) if a.split == 'dev' else
                np.arange(8, 1024, 16) if a.split == 'held' else
                np.sort(np.r_[np.arange(0, 1024, 16), np.arange(8, 1024, 16)]),
                np.arange(1024, 1030)]
    W = load(ids); results = []
    for n, K in [tuple(map(int, s.split(':'))) for s in a.settings.split(',')]:
        start = time.time(); est, cost = mf3(W, n, K)
        row = score(est, ids, cost); row.update(n=n, K=K)
        results.append(row)
        Path(a.out).parent.mkdir(exist_ok=True)
        Path(a.out).write_text(json.dumps(results, indent=2))
        print(n, K, 'seconds', round(time.time()-start, 1),
              {k: v for k, v in row.items() if k not in
               ('ids', 'estimates', 'errors_pp')}, flush=True)

if __name__ == '__main__':
    with threadpool_limits(limits=2):
        main()
```

```python
"""Deterministic event-branch quadrature, optional age/value-bin mean merging.
The ninth-step mixture is integrated exactly over the final Gaussian input.
This is a point/mean closure, not an exact affine-component distribution law.
"""
import argparse, json, time
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
from scipy.special import ndtr, ndtri
from threadpoolctl import threadpool_limits
from r6_core import load, rule, score

def phi(x):
    return np.exp(-x*x/2)/np.sqrt(2*np.pi)

def last_input(W, h, M, S, ip, jp):
    """Exact gate sweep with lowest-index output ties. No random numbers."""
    probs = np.zeros(h.shape[:2]); B = len(M); rows = np.arange(B)
    for n, (wi, wh, wo) in enumerate(zip(*W)):
        a = h[n] @ wh.T
        gates = np.divide(-a, wi, out=np.full_like(a, np.inf), where=wi != 0)
        bounds = np.column_stack([gates, S, M])
        order = np.argsort(bounds, axis=1)
        bounds = np.take_along_axis(bounds, order, axis=1)
        on = (wi < 0) | ((wi == 0) & (a > 0))
        c = (a*on) @ wo.T
        d = (wi*on) @ wo.T
        low = np.full(B, -np.inf); total = np.zeros(B)
        for k in range(19):
            high = bounds[:, k] if k < 18 else np.full(B, np.inf)
            tgt = np.where(low >= M, ip, np.where(low >= S, 9, jp))
            dc = c[rows, tgt, None]-c
            dd = d[rows, tgt, None]-d
            roots = np.divide(-dc, dd, out=np.zeros_like(dd), where=dd != 0)
            l = np.maximum(low, np.max(np.where(dd > 0, roots, -np.inf), axis=1))
            u = np.minimum(high, np.min(np.where(dd < 0, roots, np.inf), axis=1))
            bad = np.any((dd == 0) & ((dc < 0) | ((dc == 0) &
                          (np.arange(10)[None, :] < tgt[:, None]))), axis=1)
            total += np.where((u > l) & ~bad, ndtr(u)-ndtr(l), 0)
            if k < 18:
                gate = order[:, k]; j = np.minimum(gate, 15)
                sign = np.where(gate < 16, np.sign(wi[j]), 0)
                c += (sign*a[rows, j])[:, None]*wo[:, j].T
                d += (sign*wi[j])[:, None]*wo[:, j].T
                flip = (gate < 16) & (wi[j] != 0)
                on[rows[flip], j[flip]] ^= True
                # Subtracting gate contributions can leave roundoff when the
                # exact hidden state is zero. Restore the exact all-zero tie.
                empty = ~on.any(axis=1)
                c[empty] = 0; d[empty] = 0
            low = high
        probs[n] = total
    # Includes final recurrent map, both initial readouts, gate sorting/updates,
    # and 200 scalar-equivalents per root/CDF interval; conservatively charged.
    cost = B*(256+320+16+128+18*(40+20)+19*200)/2880
    return probs, cost

def merge(h, M, S, ip, jp, weights, bins):
    if bins == 0:
        return h, M, S, ip, jp, weights, 0.0
    keys = np.column_stack([ip, jp, np.minimum(bins-1, (bins*ndtr(M)).astype(int)),
                             np.minimum(bins-1, (bins*ndtr(S)).astype(int))])
    _, inv = np.unique(keys, axis=0, return_inverse=True)
    ix = np.argsort(inv, kind='stable')
    starts = np.r_[0, np.flatnonzero(np.diff(inv[ix]))+1]
    total = np.add.reduceat(weights[ix], starts)
    merged = np.add.reduceat(h[:, ix]*weights[None, ix, None], starts, axis=1)
    merged /= total[None, :, None]
    avg = lambda x: np.add.reduceat(x[ix]*weights[ix], starts)/total
    first = ix[starts]
    cost = (32*len(weights)+16*len(total))/2880
    return merged, avg(M), avg(S), ip[first], jp[first], total, cost

def event(W, q=1, bins=0):
    """q=1 uses exact truncated means; q>1 Gauss-Legendre in conditional CDF.
    bins=0 retains every component. Positive bins merge joint hidden vectors
    by the two positions and CDF bins of M,S, keeping the entire vector mean.
    """
    nodes, weights = rule(q)
    u = np.r_[nodes/2, .5+nodes/2]
    x = np.r_[-np.sqrt(2/np.pi), np.sqrt(2/np.pi)] if q == 1 else ndtri(u)
    mass = np.tile(weights/2, 2)
    wi, wh, wo = W
    h = np.maximum(wi[:, None, :]*x[None, :, None], 0)
    M = x.copy(); S = np.full(len(x), -np.inf)
    ip = np.zeros(len(x), dtype=int); jp = np.full(len(x), -1, dtype=int)
    cost = len(x)*32/2880; counts = [len(x)]
    for t in range(1, 9):
        bounds = np.column_stack([np.zeros(len(M)), ndtr(S), ndtr(M), np.ones(len(M))])
        widths = np.diff(bounds, axis=1)
        parent, branch = np.where(widths > 1e-15)
        left = bounds[parent, branch]; width = widths[parent, branch]
        if q == 1:
            z = (phi(ndtri(left))-phi(ndtri(left+width)))/width
            w = mass[parent]*width
        else:
            z = ndtri(left[:, None]+width[:, None]*nodes).ravel()
            w = (mass[parent, None]*width[:, None]*weights).ravel()
            parent = np.repeat(parent, q); branch = np.repeat(branch, q)
        h = np.maximum(h[:, parent] @ wh.transpose(0, 2, 1)
                       + wi[:, None, :]*z[None, :, None], 0)
        cost += len(z)*(272+32)/2880
        newmax = branch == 2; newsecond = branch == 1
        S = np.where(newmax, M[parent], np.where(newsecond, z, S[parent]))
        M = np.maximum(M[parent], z)
        jp = np.where(newmax, ip[parent], np.where(newsecond, t, jp[parent]))
        ip = np.where(newmax, t, ip[parent]); mass = w
        h, M, S, ip, jp, mass, c = merge(h, M, S, ip, jp, mass, bins)
        cost += c; counts.append(len(M))
    p, c = last_input(W, h, M, S, ip, jp)
    assert abs(mass.sum()-1) < 1e-10
    return p@mass, cost+c, counts

def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument('--split', choices=['dev', 'held'], default='dev')
    ap.add_argument('--settings', default='1:1,1:2,1:4,1:8,1:0,2:2,2:4,2:8')
    ap.add_argument('--out', default='r6_results/event_dev.json')
    a = ap.parse_args()
    ids = np.r_[np.arange(0 if a.split == 'dev' else 8, 1024, 16), np.arange(1024, 1030)]
    W = load(ids); out = []
    for q, bins in [tuple(map(int, s.split(':'))) for s in a.settings.split(',')]:
        start = time.time(); est, cost, counts = event(W, q, bins)
        r = score(est, ids, cost); r.update(q=q, bins=bins, counts=counts)
        out.append(r); Path(a.out).parent.mkdir(exist_ok=True)
        Path(a.out).write_text(json.dumps(out, indent=2))
        print(q, bins, 'seconds', round(time.time()-start, 1),
              {k:v for k,v in r.items() if k not in ('ids','estimates','errors_pp')}, flush=True)

if __name__ == '__main__':
    with threadpool_limits(limits=2):
        main()
```

```python
"""MF3 anchor: tensor Beta quadrature and exact Gaussian S-line integration.

Uses the supplied lines.trace_line; reports its measured matrix/readout MAC
count (2,880 MACs/forward), a lower bound excluding roots, sorting and CDF work.
No assumed prefix-tree acceleration. Failed overflow attempts are also charged.
"""
import os
for _name in ("OMP_NUM_THREADS", "OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS", "MKL_NUM_THREADS", "NUMBA_NUM_THREADS"):
    os.environ[_name] = "2"
import argparse
import json
import time
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
import numba
from scipy.special import ndtri
from lines import trace_line
from r6_core import ARR, load, rule, forward, score


def nodes(n):
    ur, wr = rule(n,8,3)
    ug, wg = rule(n,2,1)
    out = []
    for a,wa in zip(ur,wr):
        for g,wb in zip(ug,wg):
            b = a+(1-a)*g
            R,M = ndtri(a),ndtri(b)
            mu = -np.exp(-R*R/2)/np.sqrt(2*np.pi)/a
            x = np.full((len(ARR),10),mu)
            x[np.arange(len(ARR)),ARR[:,0]] = M
            x[np.arange(len(ARR)),ARR[:,1]] = R
            x[np.arange(len(ARR)),ARR[:,2]] = R
            out.append((x,R,M,float(wa*wb),float(a),float(b)))
    return out


def exact_node(W,x,R,M,cap=512):
    remaining = np.arange(len(x))
    prob = np.empty(len(x))
    total_cost = 0.0
    discarded = 0
    p = np.ascontiguousarray(ARR[:,1],dtype=np.int64)
    while len(remaining):
        pp,cc,overflow = trace_line(*W,x[remaining],p[remaining],
            np.full(len(remaining),R),np.full(len(remaining),M),p[remaining],cap)
        total_cost += float(cc.sum())
        good = overflow == 0
        if np.any((pp[good] < -1e-10) | (pp[good] > 1+1e-10)):
            raise ArithmeticError("Invalid exact line probability")
        prob[remaining[good]] = pp[good]
        remaining = remaining[~good]
        discarded += len(remaining)
        if len(remaining):
            cap *= 2
    return prob,total_cost,discarded,cap


def mf3_exact(W,n,cap=512):
    est,cost,discarded,maxcap = 0.0,0.0,0,cap
    for x,R,M,w,a,b in nodes(n):
        p,c,d,used = exact_node(W,x,R,M,cap)
        est += w*p.mean()
        cost += c
        discarded += d
        maxcap = max(maxcap,used)
    return est,cost,discarded,maxcap


def verify(W,n=3,K=4096,cap=512):
    """One central outer node, all720 lines, checked against a dense CDF grid."""
    x,R,M,w,a,b = nodes(n)[n*n//2]
    exact,c,d,used = exact_node(W,x,R,M,cap)
    s = ndtri(a+(b-a)*(np.arange(K)+.5)/K)
    dense = np.empty(len(ARR))
    for start in range(0,len(ARR),8):
        part = np.arange(start,min(start+8,len(ARR)))
        xx = np.repeat(x[part,None,:],K,axis=1)
        xx[np.arange(len(part)),:,ARR[part,1]] = s
        logits = forward([v[None] for v in W],xx.reshape(-1,10))[0]
        dense[part] = (logits.argmax(1).reshape(len(part),K)==ARR[part,1,None]).mean(1)
    return dict(n=n,K=K,lines=len(ARR),mean_error_pp=float(100*(dense-exact).mean()),
                max_abs_error_pp=float(100*np.max(np.abs(dense-exact))),
                rms_error_pp=float(100*np.sqrt(np.mean((dense-exact)**2))),
                exact_node_mac_forwards=c,discarded_lines=d,maxcap=used)


def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument("--split",choices=("dev","held"),default="dev")
    ap.add_argument("--orders",default="3,4")
    ap.add_argument("--dev-count",type=int,default=64)
    ap.add_argument("--maxp",type=int,default=512)
    ap.add_argument("--verify",action="store_true")
    ap.add_argument("--resume",action="store_true")
    ap.add_argument("--out",default="r6_results/exact_anchor_dev.json")
    a = ap.parse_args()
    pop = np.arange(0 if a.split=="dev" else 8,1024,16)[:a.dev_count]
    ids = np.r_[pop,np.arange(1024,1030)]
    W = load(ids)
    result = []
    output = Path(a.out)
    output.parent.mkdir(exist_ok=True)
    if a.resume and output.exists():
        result = json.loads(output.read_text())
        if any(row["ids"] != ids.tolist() for row in result):
            raise ValueError("Cannot resume a different network cohort")
    if a.verify:
        v = verify([w[0] for w in W],cap=a.maxp)
        output.with_suffix(".verification.json").write_text(json.dumps(v,indent=2))
        print("verification",v,flush=True)
    for n in map(int,a.orders.split(",")):
        if any(row["n"]==n for row in result):
            continue
        start = time.time()
        est,cost,discarded,maxcaps = [],[],[],[]
        for i in range(len(ids)):
            e,c,d,m = mf3_exact([w[i] for w in W],n,a.maxp)
            est.append(e); cost.append(c); discarded.append(d); maxcaps.append(m)
            if i%8==0:

                      "seconds",round(time.time()-start,1),flush=True)
        est,cost = np.array(est),np.array(cost)
        row = score(est,ids,float(cost[:len(pop)].mean()))
        row.update(n=n,cost_is_lower_bound=True,cost_definition=__doc__,
                   costs_per_network=cost.tolist(),discarded_lines=discarded,maxcaps=maxcaps,
                   seconds=time.time()-start)
        ref = np.load("pop1k/reference.npz")
        pq = ref["acc"][ids]*(1-ref["acc"][ids])
        sample = float(100*np.sqrt(np.mean(pq[:len(pop)]/cost[:len(pop)])))
        metric = row[a.split]
        metric.update(sampling_at_network_costs=sample,
                      ratio_at_network_costs=metric["rms"]**2/sample**2,

                      anchor_rms_pp=float(100*np.sqrt(np.mean((est[:len(pop)]-ref["mf3"][pop])**2))))
        result.append(row)
        output.write_text(json.dumps(result,indent=2))
```

```python
"""Deterministic two-level MF3/MF4 correction; RNG is confined to measure().
Run from the repository, with OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2. All forward rows charged.
Examples: python r6_correction.py --measure 256
 python r6_correction.py --split dev --k 3 --n 2 --K 16 --q 8 --keep 8
 python r6_correction.py --split held --k 4 --n 2 --K 8 --q 8 --keep 8
"""
import argparse, itertools, json, os, pathlib, time
import numpy as np
from scipy.special import ndtr, ndtri, roots_jacobi

OUT = pathlib.Path('r6_results'); OUT.mkdir(exist_ok=True)

def save_npz(path, **data):
    """Atomic replacement also keeps any open lazy NPZ reader valid."""
    tmp=path.with_name(path.name+f'.{os.getpid()}.tmp')
    with tmp.open('wb') as f: np.savez_compressed(f,**data)
    tmp.replace(path)

def load(split='dev'):
    ids = np.r_[np.arange(8 if split == 'held' else 0, 1024, 16), 1024:1030]
    p = np.load('pop1k/seed0.npz')
    z = [np.load(f) for f in ['weights.npz']+[f'weights_seed{i}.npz' for i in range(5)]]
    zz = [np.stack([v[key][:, 0] if j == 0 else v[key] for v in z]) for j, key in
          enumerate(['rnn.weight_ih_l0', 'rnn.weight_hh_l0', 'linear.weight'])]
    w = [np.concatenate([p['s1048576_'+key][ids[:-6]], b]).astype(np.float32) for key, b in zip(['Wih','Whh','Wo'],zz)]
    return ids, w

def predict(w, x):
    h = np.zeros((len(w[0]), len(x), 16), dtype=w[0].dtype)
    for t in range(10): h = np.maximum(h @ w[1].transpose(0,2,1) + x[None,:,t,None]*w[0][:,None], 0)
    return (h @ w[2].transpose(0,2,1)).argmax(-1)

def rule(n, a, b):
    x, w = roots_jacobi(n, b-1, a-1)
    return (x+1)/2, w/w.sum()

def mu(v): return -np.exp(-np.asarray(v)**2/2)/np.sqrt(2*np.pi)/ndtr(v)

def arrangements(k, keep):
    a = np.array(list(itertools.permutations(range(10), k)))
    if keep == 0 or keep == (8 if k == 3 else 56): return a
    chosen = []
    for row in a:
        f = [x for x in range(10) if x not in row[:2]]
        if k == 3: yes = f.index(row[2]) in {1:[3],2:[1,6],4:[0,2,5,7]}[keep]
        else:
            pairs = [(0,7),(7,0),(1,3),(3,1),(2,5),(5,2),(4,6),(6,4)]
            yes = (f.index(row[2]),f.index(row[3])) in pairs[:keep]
        if yes: chosen.append(row)
    return np.array(chosen)

def grid(w, k, n, K, q, keep):
    """Returns per-arrangement integrals: anchor3, anchor-k, corrected model.
    CDF Gauss-Jacobi outer rule, S midpoints, q Gauss-Legendre lower nodes.
    For k=4 the separately evaluated MF3 anchor allows MF4-model minus MF3.
    """
    arr = arrangements(k, keep); A = len(arr); N = len(w[0]); low = np.array([[i for i in range(10) if i not in a] for a in arr])
    rules = [rule(n,8,3),rule(n,2,1)] if k == 3 else [rule(n,7,4),rule(n,1,3),rule(n,2,1)]
    uq, wq = rule(q,1,1); out = np.zeros((N,A,3)); rows = 1+(10-k)*q+(k==4)
    block = max(1,4096//(K*rows)); count = 0
    for ij in itertools.product(range(n), repeat=len(rules)):
        u = [rules[t][0][j] for t,j in enumerate(ij)]; wt = np.prod([rules[t][1][j] for t,j in enumerate(ij)])
        if k == 3: fr = u[0]; fm = fr+(1-fr)*u[1]; vals = [ndtri(fm),None,ndtri(fr)]
        else: fq=u[0]; fr=fq+(1-fq)*u[1]; fm=fr+(1-fr)*u[2]; vals=[ndtri(fm),None,ndtri(fr),ndtri(fq)]
        s = ndtri(fr+(fm-fr)*(np.arange(K)+.5)/K); zn = ndtri(ndtr(vals[-1])*uq)
        for start in range(0,A,block):
            aa = arr[start:start+block]; ll = low[start:start+block]; b=len(aa)
            base = np.full((b,K,10),mu(vals[-1]),np.float32)
            for j,v in enumerate(vals): base[np.arange(b)[:,None],np.arange(K),aa[:,j,None]] = s if j==1 else v
            X = np.repeat(base[:,:,None,:],rows,axis=2)
            for j in range(10-k): X[np.arange(b)[:,None,None],np.arange(K)[None,:,None],1+j*q+np.arange(q),ll[:,j,None,None]] = zn
            if k == 4:
                X[:,:,-1] = mu(vals[2])
                for j in range(3): X[np.arange(b)[:,None],np.arange(K),-1,aa[:,j,None]] = s if j==1 else vals[j]
            I = (predict(w,X.reshape(-1,10)).reshape(N,b,K,rows)==aa[None,:,None,None,1]).astype(float)
            i0=I[...,0]; ej=(I[...,1:1+(10-k)*q].reshape(N,b,K,10-k,q)*wq).sum(-1)
            model=np.clip(i0+(ej-i0[...,None]).sum(-1),0,1)
            out[:,start:start+b,0]+=wt*(I[...,-1] if k==4 else i0).mean(-1)
            out[:,start:start+b,1]+=wt*i0.mean(-1); out[:,start:start+b,2]+=wt*model.mean(-1)
            count += b*K*rows
    return arr,out,count

def score(ids, est, rows, target='acc'):
    p=np.load('pop1k/reference.npz'); e=100*(est-p[target][ids]); pop=e[:-6]
    samp=100*np.sqrt(np.mean(p['acc'][ids[:-6]]*(1-p['acc'][ids[:-6]]))/(rows*1.01))
    return dict(forward_rows=int(rows),cost=rows*1.01,rms=float(np.sqrt(np.mean(pop**2))),mean=float(pop.mean()),
                sampling_rms=float(samp),mse_ratio=float(np.mean(pop**2)/samp**2),zoo=e[-6:].tolist())

def measure(reps):
    """Balanced independent measurement of true-minus-anchor arrangement means.
    This function never constructs an estimator; finite measurement noise is subtracted.
    """
    ids,w=load(); arr=arrangements(4,0); N=len(ids); A=len(arr); rng=np.random.default_rng(6007)
    sums=np.zeros((2,N,A)); squares=np.zeros_like(sums); parts=np.zeros((4,2,N,A))
    block=max(1,4096//reps); t0=time.time()
    for st in range(0,A,block):
        aa=arr[st:st+block]; b=len(aa); a=np.repeat(aa,reps,axis=0); B=len(a)
        v=np.sort(rng.standard_normal((B,10)),axis=1)[:,::-1][:,:4]
        lp=np.array([[p for p in range(10) if p not in row] for row in a]); x=np.zeros((B,10),np.float32)
        x[np.arange(B)[:,None],lp]=ndtri(ndtr(v[:,3,None])*rng.random((B,6)))
        x[np.arange(B)[:,None],a]=v; y=a[:,1]; truth=predict(w,x)==y
        for j,k in enumerate((3,4)):
            base=np.repeat(mu(v[:,k-1,None]),10,axis=1).astype(np.float32); base[np.arange(B)[:,None],a[:,:k]]=v[:,:k]
            d=(truth.astype(np.int8)-(predict(w,base)==y)).reshape(N,b,reps)
            sums[j,:,st:st+b]=d.sum(-1); squares[j,:,st:st+b]=(d*d).sum(-1)
            for h,pp in enumerate(np.array_split(np.arange(reps),4)): parts[h,j,:,st:st+b]=d[...,pp].sum(-1)
        if st%(block*80)==0: print('measurement',st,A,round(time.time()-t0,1),flush=True)
    result={}
    for j,k in enumerate((3,4)):
        groups=np.array([tuple(row[:k]) for row in arr]); _,inv=np.unique(groups,axis=0,return_inverse=True)
        G=inv.max()+1; s=np.zeros((N,G)); ss=np.zeros_like(s); nr=np.bincount(inv)*reps
        for i in range(N): s[i]=np.bincount(inv,weights=sums[j,i]); ss[i]=np.bincount(inv,weights=squares[j,i])
        means=s/nr; rawnoise=(squares[j]-sums[j]**2/reps)/(reps-1)/reps
        noise=np.stack([np.bincount(inv,weights=v) for v in rawnoise])/(nr/reps)**2
        var=means.var(1)-(1-1/G)*noise.mean(1)
        result[f'mf{k}']=dict(sd_points=(100*np.sqrt(np.maximum(var,0))).tolist(),raw_sd_points=(100*means.std(1)).tolist(),
            per_arrangement_se_points=(100*np.sqrt(noise.mean(1))).tolist(),correction_mean_points=(100*means.mean(1)).tolist(),arrangements=int(G),draws_per_arrangement=int(nr[0]))
    save_npz(OUT/'correction_measurement.npz',ids=ids,arr=arr,sums=sums,squares=squares,parts=parts,reps=reps)
    (OUT/'correction_measurement.json').write_text(json.dumps(result,indent=2)); arrangement_report()

        arr,inv=np.unique(a[:,:k],axis=0,return_inverse=True); G=len(arr); N=len(z['ids']); nr=np.bincount(inv)*reps
        s=np.stack([np.bincount(inv,weights=v,minlength=G) for v in z['sums'][j]])
        ss=np.stack([np.bincount(inv,weights=v,minlength=G) for v in z['squares'][j]])
```

```python
"""Measurement-only gate-history diagnostic; not an accuracy estimator.

The first half of independent iid Gaussian inputs discovers patterns; the second
half measures mass coverage. Error fragments also fix top-two and output indices,
so they form a particular convex-cone partition, not maximal convex error pieces.
"""
import os
for _name in ("OMP_NUM_THREADS", "OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS", "MKL_NUM_THREADS"):
    os.environ[_name] = "2"
import argparse
import json
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np


def keys(gates, group=None):
    """Opaque exact gate bitstrings, optionally prefixed by a grouping label."""
    raw = np.ascontiguousarray(gates).view(np.dtype((np.void, gates.shape[1]*2))).ravel()
    if group is None:
        return raw
    out = np.empty(len(raw), dtype=[("group", "u4"), ("pattern", raw.dtype)])
    out["group"], out["pattern"] = group, raw
    return out


def species(key, split, keep=None):
    """Discovery/test counts and held-out coverage of discovered species."""
    if keep is None:
        keep = np.ones(len(key), bool)
    which = np.arange(len(key))[keep] < split
    unique, inverse = np.unique(key[keep], return_inverse=True)
    a = np.bincount(inverse[which], minlength=len(unique))
    b = np.bincount(inverse[~which], minlength=len(unique))
    na, nb = int(a.sum()), int(b.sum())
    result = dict(observed_species=len(unique), discovery_species=int((a>0).sum()),
                  discovery_rows=na, test_rows=nb,
                  unseen_test_mass=float(b[a==0].sum()/max(1,nb)))
    order = np.argsort(-a, kind="stable")
    result["top_k_test_mass"] = {
        str(k): float(b[order[:min(k,int((a>0).sum()))]].sum()/max(1,nb))
        for k in (1,16,64,256,1024,4096)
    }
    if unique.dtype.names is not None:
        group = unique["group"]
        order = np.lexsort((-a, group))
        gs = group[order]
        starts = np.r_[0, np.flatnonzero(gs[1:] != gs[:-1])+1]
        rank = np.arange(len(order)) - np.repeat(starts, np.diff(np.r_[starts,len(order)]))
        result["observed_groups"] = len(starts)
        result["top_k_per_group_test_mass"] = {
            str(k): float(b[order[(rank<k)&(a[order]>0)]].sum()/max(1,nb))
            for k in (1,4,16)
        }
    return result


def input_groups(x, steps):
    M = np.full(len(x), -np.inf)
    S = M.copy()
    history = np.zeros(len(x), np.uint32)
    result = {}
    for t in range(10):
        nm, ns = x[:,t] > M, x[:,t] > S
        event = np.where(nm,2,np.where(ns,1,0))
        history = 3*history + event.astype(np.uint32)
        S, M = np.where(nm,M,np.where(ns,x[:,t],S)), np.maximum(M,x[:,t])
        if t+1 in steps:
            top = np.argsort(x[:,:t+1],axis=1)[:,-2:]
            result[t+1] = ((t-top[:,1])*(t+1)+(t-top[:,0]), history.copy())
    return result


def load_models(root, count):
    p = np.load(root/"pop1k/seed0.npz")
    w = [p["s1048576_"+k].astype(np.float64) for k in ("Wih","Whh","Wo")]
    indices = np.arange(0,64,max(1,64//count))[:count]*16
    for i in indices:
        yield "pop"+str(i), [v[i] for v in w]
    for name, file in [("mystery","weights.npz")]+[(f"seed{i}",f"weights_seed{i}.npz") for i in range(5)]:
        p = np.load(root/file)
        yield name, [p["rnn.weight_ih_l0"][:,0].astype(float),
                     p["rnn.weight_hh_l0"].astype(float), p["linear.weight"].astype(float)]


def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument("--root",type=Path,default=Path(__file__).resolve().parent)
    ap.add_argument("--dev-count",type=int,default=8,choices=(8,16,32,64))
    ap.add_argument("--log2-inputs",type=int,default=16)
    ap.add_argument("--seed",type=int,default=2026101906)
    ap.add_argument("--out",type=Path,default=Path("r6_geometry.json"))
    args = ap.parse_args()
    n = 2**args.log2_inputs
    x = np.random.default_rng(args.seed).standard_normal((n,10))
    top = np.argsort(x,axis=1)[:,-2:]
    target, maxpos = top[:,0], top[:,1]
    steps = (3,5,7,9,10)
    groups = input_groups(x,steps)
    result = dict(description=__doc__, seed=args.seed, inputs=n, discovery_inputs=n//2,
                  dev_count=args.dev_count, measurement_forwards_per_network=n, networks=[])
    for name,(wi,wh,wo) in load_models(args.root,args.dev_count):
        h = np.zeros((n,16))
        gates = np.empty((n,10),np.uint16)
        bit = (1 << np.arange(16)).astype(np.uint16)
        for t in range(10):
            h = h@wh.T+x[:,t,None]*wi
            gates[:,t] = ((h>0)*bit).sum(1,dtype=np.uint16)
            np.maximum(h,0,out=h)
        pred = (h@wo.T).argmax(1)
        error = pred != target
        row = dict(name=name, accuracy=float(1-error.mean()), prefixes={})
        for t in steps:
```

```python
"""Small regression checks only; randomized fixtures never enter an estimator."""
import os
for _name in ("OMP_NUM_THREADS", "OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS", "MKL_NUM_THREADS", "NUMBA_NUM_THREADS"):
    os.environ[_name] = "2"
import argparse
import json

import numba
from scipy.special import ndtr
from r6_core import load, forward, prefix_forward
from r6_event import last_input
from r6_exact_anchor import nodes, exact_node
from lines import trace_line


def verify():
    W = load(np.array([0,1024]))
    tokens = np.array([[0]*10,[0]*9+[1],[0]*8+[1,2],
        [0,1,2,0,1,2,0,1,2,0],[0,1,2,0,1,2,0,1,2,0],
        [1,0,2,2,0,1,1,2,0,0]])
    values = np.array([-1.25,.125,1.5])
    direct = forward(W,values[tokens])
    shared,cost = prefix_forward(W,tokens,values)
    assert np.allclose(direct,shared,rtol=1e-12,atol=1e-10)
    result = dict(prefix_max_logit_difference=float(np.max(np.abs(direct-shared))),
                  prefix_counted_cost=float(cost),direct_rows=len(tokens))

    # Independent Gaussian prefixes are a measurement fixture, not an estimator.
    x = np.zeros((6,10))
    x[:,:9] = np.random.default_rng(2026101907).standard_normal((6,9))
    top = np.argsort(x[:,:9],axis=1)[:,-2:]
    jp,ip = top[:,0],top[:,1]
    S,M = x[np.arange(6),jp],x[np.arange(6),ip]
    h = np.zeros((len(W[0]),6,16))
    for t in range(9):
        h = np.maximum(h@W[1].transpose(0,2,1)+W[0][:,None,:]*x[None,:,t,None],0)
    swept,_ = last_input(W,h,M,S,ip,jp)
    xx = np.repeat(x,3,axis=0)
    lo = np.column_stack([np.full(6,-12.),S,M]).ravel()
    hi = np.column_stack([S,M,np.full(6,12.)]).ravel()
    target = np.column_stack([jp,np.full(6,9),ip]).ravel().astype(np.int64)
    p = np.full(len(xx),9,np.int64)
    checked = []
    for i in range(len(W[0])):
        probability,_,overflow = trace_line(*[w[i] for w in W],xx,p,lo,hi,target)
        assert not overflow.any()
        checked.append((probability*(ndtr(hi)-ndtr(lo))).reshape(6,3).sum(1))
    checked = np.array(checked)
    # The omitted Gaussian tails beyond +/-12 have total mass below 4e-33.
    assert np.allclose(swept,checked,rtol=1e-10,atol=1e-10)
    result["last_input_max_probability_difference"] = float(np.max(np.abs(swept-checked)))

    wi = np.tile([1.,-1.],8)
    wh = np.zeros((16,16)); wh[:,0] = -1
    wo = np.random.default_rng(0).standard_normal((10,16)); wo[0] = 0
    hh = np.zeros((1,1,16)); hh[0,0,0] = 1
    probability,_ = last_input([wi[None],wh[None],wo[None]],hh,
        np.array([3.]),np.array([2.]),np.array([1]),np.array([0]))
    expected = float(ndtr(1)-ndtr(-1))
    assert abs(float(probability[0,0])-expected)<1e-14
    result["all_off_probability"] = float(probability[0,0])
    result["all_off_expected"] = expected

    x,R,M,_,_,_ = nodes(3)[4]
    ww = [w[0] for w in W]
    a,c,_,_ = exact_node(ww,x,R,M,512)
    b,retry_cost,discarded,capacity = exact_node(ww,x,R,M,4)
    assert np.array_equal(a,b) and discarded>0 and retry_cost>c
    result["overflow_retry"] = dict(max_probability_difference=float(np.max(np.abs(a-b))),
        baseline_mac_forwards=c,with_retries_mac_forwards=retry_cost,
        discarded_lines=discarded,final_capacity=capacity)
    return result


if __name__ == "__main__":
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    parser.add_argument("--out",default="r6_results/verify.json")
    args = parser.parse_args()
    numba.set_num_threads(2)
    result = verify()
    path = Path(args.out); path.parent.mkdir(exist_ok=True)
    path.write_text(json.dumps(result,indent=2))
    print(json.dumps(result,indent=2))
```

```python
"""Restricted cone-oracle bound and analytic ANOVA counterexample; no data."""
from math import atan, cos, pi, sin, sqrt


def G(r):
    a = atan(r)
    return (35*a - 28*sin(2*a) + 7*sin(4*a)
            - 4*sin(6*a)/3 + sin(8*a)/8)/(35*pi)


V = 0.069

    if G(mid) < Q:
        lo = mid
    else:
        hi = mid

print("Queries; worst-error lower(pp); sampling RMS(pp); squared ratio")
```

```python
"""Reproduce the original restricted-oracle bound in r6_bounds.md.

No data access, fitting, random numbers, or external packages are required.
These are minimax bounds for a larger geometric class, not measurements on
the 432-parameter RNN population.
"""
from math import atan, cos, pi, sin, sqrt


def cone_mass(radius):
    """P[X_10 >= ||X_1:9|| / radius], for X ~ N(0, I_10)."""
    angle = atan(radius)
    return (35 * angle - 28 * sin(2 * angle) + 7 * sin(4 * angle)
            - (4 / 3) * sin(6 * angle) + sin(8 * angle) / 8) / (35 * pi)


def bound_constants(variance=0.069):
    cap = (1 - sqrt(1 - 4 * variance)) / 2
    lower, upper = 0.0, 10.0
    for _ in range(80):
        middle = (lower + upper) / 2
        if cone_mass(middle) < cap:
            lower = middle
        else:
            upper = middle
    return cap, (lower + upper) / 2


def lower_bound(queries, radius):
    angle = (256 / (175 * queries)) ** (1 / 8)
    return (2 / 5) * (cone_mass(radius) - cone_mass(radius * cos(angle)))


def main():
    variance = 0.069
    cap, radius = bound_constants(variance)
    print(f"Error-mass cap: {cap:.12f}; cone radius: {radius:.12f}")
    print("queries  lower_pp  sampling_pp  squared_ratio")
    for queries in [100, 1000, 10000, 100000, 1000000, 10000000]:
        lower = lower_bound(queries, radius)
        sampling = sqrt(variance / queries)
        print(f"{queries:>8} {100*lower:>10.6f} {100*sampling:>12.6f} "
              f"{(lower/sampling)**2:>14.6f}")
    print("\nIllustrative certificate radii from z26's estimated median tails:")
    print("patterns  forwards  estimated_tail_pp  midpoint_radius_pp  sampling_pp")
    for patterns, tail in [(1, .80103), (3, .60671), (10, .33667),
                           (30, .15146), (100, .04989), (300, .01299),
                           (1000, .00291)]:
        cost = 5040 * patterns
        print(f"{patterns:>8} {cost:>9} {100*tail:>18.3f} "
              f"{50*tail:>19.4f} {100*sqrt(variance/cost):>12.6f}")
    print("\nTruncation crossover from 0.362 points:", variance / .00362**2)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```
